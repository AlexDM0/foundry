Compounding: how the factory improves

Version 2 of the compounding research of 16 November, as a roadmap item: stage 2 of foundry's road to the agentic software factory. It states what the whitepaper asks, what forge records today, what it must start to gather, and what the owner decided on 23 November. Nothing on this page is built. The page presents the design document;[1] where the two differ, the document is right.

The whitepaper asks

Measure, propose, a person decides

The factory learns from its own finished work: it measures features and sessions, proposes improvements, tries them on past features when wanted, and applies nothing by itself.[11]

Foundry starts

The raw data is already kept

Every job session's stream sits in its job folder with a stamp per line, and nothing deletes it. Since the rework the log also holds one record per pipeline step, with its run time.[2][3]

The gap

The joins, and a job that does not land

A gate step names no job. The pipeline a job ran under is kept nowhere. Only totals reach the log. And nothing can run a job whose result stays off the main line.

Two rules hold through all of it. A figure is computed by code from what forge recorded, never by a model. And a proposal changes nothing: accepting it starts the path that kind of change already has.

Version 1 was written against the Overseer, the Watcher and the Bridge. Version 2 has two inputs it did not have: the codebase after the rework, read at commit 0f1e760f, and the whitepaper of 20 November. The owner answered this version's questions on 23 November; they are under Decisions. The last table lists what changed.

Where foundry starts

Read from the code on 23 November 2026, at commit 0f1e760f. Forge is headless now: Hephaestus and Talos run the flow, Gaia is the window, a store port holds the records, and entities are built.[9]

The figures, and where each comes from

"Recorded" means a stored record holds it today. "Derived" means code can compute it today from kept files. "In part" means a record holds it without something the figure needs, mostly the job it belongs to. "Gap" means forge does not record it.

Entities

  • Entities are built. A job carries its entity and its stage, and job-launched records both, so every figure of a job groups by entity.
  • A step record names the entity whose gate it is. A package build runs its dependents' gates as well, so that entity is often not the job's.
  • A crossing ticket spends tokens under several entities. They count for the entity of each stage's job, not for the ticket's home.
  • Each entity has its own constitution, gates, review settings, agent settings and concurrency, so each has its own proposals, experiments and ledger. The product owner, the pipeline agent and the Assistant are the forge's.

What the tokens were spent on

No standard taxonomy exists; version 1's research found none. Two deterministic views are proposed, read from the event log and the files of the job folder.

  • By activity: when in the job. Orient, build, self-check, repair, catch-up, report, with boundaries the event log shows. Version 1 called this "phase"; that word belongs to the pipeline now.
  • By content: what filled the context. Instruction files, the ticket, files read per path, search results, shell output, the red-handoff note, subagent results, the session's own output. An item costs its size once when it enters, and again at each later call.

What to gather

Six things forge must start to record. Everything else is derived. Version 1 asked for a seventh, the ticket as launched, and the rework closed it.

  1. The job on each gate step Smaller than in v1

    The rework records pipeline-step-ran: step kind, name, phase, entity, run time and ending. What is left is the join: the job and its tickets, the place the step ran, the tree, the exit code, the wait for a gate slot, the cache report, the child instance, and one output file per red step in place of one gate.log that the next red overwrites.[2][5]

    GivesFirst-handoff pass rate per gate, flaky gates, gate time per feature.

  2. The reason of a red handoff, in the log Smaller than in v1

    red-handoff.json already holds what went red: its kind, the gate's name or the Customs check, the exit code and the resume count. It is a file of the job folder, and the next red overwrites it. job-interrupted gains those fields, and a job that Customs ends gains the check's id.[4]

    GivesA session that fails the same check in every job points at a brief; one that fails the same gate points at the gate.

  3. The configuration a job ran under As in v1

    The content hash of the pipeline on job-launched; each stored pipeline kept by that hash; each instruction file's content kept by the hash the launch already records. Today pipeline.json is overwritten, and an Apply logs the names of the changed fields and not their values.[6]

    GivesA range of time that splits by configuration, and a ledger that can ask whether a figure moved after a change.

  4. The ticket as launched Closed by the rework

    A committed ticket's title and body are kept under their content hash, written once and never rewritten, and a job's start.json names the hash. A ticket holds no picture: User's pictures go to the product owner only, kept under the hash of their bytes for as long as a log record names them.[7]

  5. A mark on everything an experiment writes As in v1

    Every job, gate step and token record of an experiment carries its experiment id and its repetition. No experiment credits a row, counts toward a ticket's bounds or appears in a figure of real work. Compounding's own spend is a figure of its own.

  6. Compounding's own records Wider than in v1

    A run started and ended; a proposal and its fate, with who decided; what an accepted proposal became (a pipeline revision, a ticket, a constitution commit); an experiment with the phase it studies and its share of the launch slots; a person's eval; a quality agent's verdict; and a grading of an accepted change by a later run.

  7. The session measurements the log drops New

    At a job's end the log keeps three token counts and a cost. The stream holds more: cache creation apart from input, thinking, the totals per model, the vendor's turn count and API time, the peak context. The agent port gains one capability, the measurements of a session, and the digest is built from its answer.[3]

Where the records live

  • Behind the store port. Every stored record of compounding is a record of BoardStore with a kind of its own. No code outside the local adapter names its path.[8]
  • Raw logs stay local. Event logs, worktrees and job folders are outside the store by design. So the forge that ran a job writes its digest when the job ends.
  • Served, not read. Gaia gets figures, proposals and experiments as documents of the Hephaestus API and starts a run through a command. Cost joins the served documents here for the first time.
What gathering costs. A log record of an unknown kind, or with an unknown word of a stored vocabulary, refuses the whole log, so an older program cannot read a log that holds the first new record. New records, documents and commands only add, so the system version's minor increases; it is 4.0 today. The rework's rule holds: nothing that exists has to be kept.
Retention. Job folders are still kept without a bound. The owner's rule: keep the raw logs of the newest 100, a count and never an age, as a setting of the board. The digest, the gate step records and the launch files are small and stay for good, so the digest must be written before the raw logs can go.

Metrics

A metric maps one feature record to one number, and it is deterministic. The owner decided that on 16 November and confirmed on 23 November that forge starts there.

The contract, proposed

A metric is a command line, as a gate is, so forge knows no language. It gets the feature record and read access to the start tree and the delivered tree, and writes a value and a unit. One that must run something runs confined, as a gate does.

The test suite

Each metric comes with small feature records and trees whose value is known. A change of the definition that moves a known value fails the suite. A board's metrics live in a tracked folder and land by a ticket.

One runner serves two designs

Forge runs gate lines and prepare steps confined today, and no other line. The phases-and-hooks design needs the same for a command hook: a confined, bounded line on a snapshot.[10]

Judged metrics are reserved for later. The whitepaper's three are an agent's score of a feature against a brief. What this version found is kept for the one that takes them up: a judged value is not a measured figure and is shown apart; a judge has a calibration set of a person's scores in place of a test suite; a judge costs about as much as a review. Nothing here blocks them: a value is stored with its metric's version, so a second kind can sit beside the first.

Experiments

An experiment is a tool. A run can end in proposals that User accepts on the figures alone. When one is wanted, it replays a delivered ticket, the case study, and User says which phase it compounds.

The subject of an experiment

The build, the plan and the spec are the owner's answers of 23 November: the spec or the plan is compounded in isolation, so the step runs alone, the experiment ends at the artifact it puts out, and the two artifacts are compared. The review is the document's row, by the same rule. The plan is a phase only once the phases-and-hooks design is built.[10] Inside its subject an experiment asks nobody: it passes an approval itself and records that, and a session that asks a question ends its repetition there.

Which tickets are candidates

One that Hephaestus delivered alone on the main line. Not: delivered by hand, in a group's release or in a bundle; a crossing ticket, whose stages ran under several entities; a ticket that declared a need, or whose group holds a fix ticket of a dependent.

What the session must not see

Replays leak: an agent in a public benchmark found the fix by listing the repository's references.[18] So a case study runs in a copy that holds the start commit's history and nothing later. Customs and confinement apply as in any job.

How far the baseline carries

The original is one run, by an older forge, pipeline and model. So a set holds one experiment with no change, the control, and each experiment runs three times, unless User sets otherwise.[17]

How a comparison is judged

  1. The metric suite. Every metric of the board grades both results. This is the score the ledger keeps.
  2. The person. The person who compares may fill in a small eval: a few criteria, a score each. It is always open: the person sees which result is the original, which the experiment made, and what was changed.
  3. A quality agent, where the board has one. It judges what no metric holds: whether the feature meets the ticket's Acceptance as well as the original does.

What it would improve

The whitepaper's six areas, and the workflow. Every change is one forge can already store, unless its row says otherwise. An accepted proposal goes the way that kind of change goes today.

Three limits. Instruction files grow, and growth costs: compounding proposes one small edit at a time, and cuts first.[19][20] A shipped brief is never edited where it is installed, so a board's change is a file of its own. And a ticket's own model is not applied by any launch, so a model per role can be stored and a model per ticket size cannot.

The pipeline agent's advise mode is the nearest thing forge has. It is still given the last 200 gate-ran lines, each an outcome and a tree, which is too little to advise from.

Proposals and versions

The whitepaper leaves who approves to the team and to company policy. Forge is one owner on one machine, so every proposal is User's to accept. The shape still fits a team later.

  • A proposal changes nothing. It is a record with its evidence. Accepting it starts a pipeline setup that User applies, a ticket that passes ticket review, or the constitution commit of an Apply. So every approval forge has also guards a compounded change.
  • A proposal names its lever. A board can then list the levers compounding may propose on, which is the whitepaper's whitelist. Proposed, not decided.
  • A proposal on the workflow is marked as one. Gates, phases, hooks and approvals are the workflow.

The audit trail is compounding's own records. For one change they answer, in order: which figures led to it, who accepted it, what it became, which configuration hashes changed with it, and whether a later run saw the figure move.

The reminder. Of the whitepaper's three ways, the owner chose two: forge reminds, and User starts the run. One notice per board, two weeks after the newest run, silent when nothing was delivered since. It launches nothing and names no command.
